Do this now

  1. If told to evacuate, leave right away. Don't wait. Wildfires move faster than you can run.
  2. If smoke is heavy, stay indoors with windows and doors closed.
  3. Run air conditioning on recirculate. Use a HEPA air purifier, or make a box fan filter (below).
  4. Outdoors, wear a well-fitted N95 or P100 respirator. Cloth and surgical masks don't block fine smoke.
  5. Avoid hard outdoor activity while smoky. Don't add indoor smoke: no candles, frying, or vacuuming.
  6. Get medical help for trouble breathing, chest pain, or a fast heartbeat.

Evacuating from a wildfire

  • Ready, Set, Go: Be ready with a packed go-bag. Get set when a fire is nearby. Go early when told to, or sooner if you feel unsafe.
  • Wear long sleeves, long pants, sturdy shoes, and an N95. Wool and cotton are safer than synthetics near fire.
  • Close all windows and doors when you leave, but leave them unlocked. Move furniture away from windows.
  • Drive with headlights on. Watch for people, animals, and emergency vehicles.
  • Don't go back until officials say it is safe.

If trapped by fire

  • In a car: park in an area clear of plants. Close windows and vents. Lie on the floor and cover yourself with a wool blanket or coat. Call 911.
  • On foot: go to an area with little fuel, like a rocky spot, a road, or a plowed field. Lie face down and cover yourself. Water is not always safe, but a pond or river can help.
  • At home: stay inside, away from outside walls. Fill sinks and tubs with water. Call 911.

DIY box fan air filter

  1. Use a 20 in (51 cm) box fan and a 20 x 20 in furnace filter rated MERV 13 or higher.
  2. Tape the filter to the back (air-intake side) of the fan. Match the arrow on the filter to the airflow direction.
  3. Run it in the room where you spend most time. Don't leave it unattended or running while you sleep unless the fan is newer and has safety features.

Who is at highest risk from smoke

Children, older adults, pregnant people, and people with asthma, COPD, or heart disease. Keep their medicines on hand and follow their action plans.

Check air quality on AirNow.gov when you have a connection. An AQI over 150 is unhealthy for everyone.
