Do this now

  1. Call 911 if you have any signal. A text to 911 may work when a call won't. Newer iPhones can text emergency services by satellite with no cell signal.
  2. Use 3 of anything to mean "help": 3 whistle blasts, 3 fires, 3 flashes, 3 piles of rocks.
  3. Blow a whistle in sets of 3 blasts, rest, and repeat. It carries farther than yelling and saves your voice.
  4. In sunlight, use a signal mirror or any shiny object to flash at aircraft, boats, or distant people.
  5. Make big ground signals in open areas with high contrast: rocks on snow, branches on sand, bright cloth on grass.
  6. Stay near your signals. Moving makes you harder to find.

Ground-to-air signals

Make them as big as you can, at least 10 ft (3 m) long, with straight lines and sharp angles. Nature has few straight lines, so they stand out.

SOSDistress, known worldwide

In daylight, an aircraft that rocks its wings from side to side is telling you it has seen you.

Signal mirror

  1. Hold the mirror near your eye. Stretch your other arm out toward the target and make a V with two fingers.
  2. Tilt the mirror until the spot of sunlight lands on your fingers.
  3. Put the target in your V. Wiggle the mirror slightly so the flash sweeps across it.
  4. Keep flashing at aircraft until they clearly signal back. Then stop. Steady flashes can blind pilots.

A mirror flash can be seen from many miles away on a clear day. Practice before you need it. A phone screen, CD, or foil can work in a pinch.

Fire and smoke

  • Three fires in a triangle or straight line are a distress signal.
  • In daytime, add green leaves or wet wood for white smoke against a dark background. Rubber or oil makes black smoke against snow. Use these only briefly and safely.
  • At night, keep a bright fire going.

Light

  • Flash a flashlight or phone light in sets of 3, or in SOS: 3 short, 3 long, 3 short.
  • Many flashlights have a strobe mode. It is very visible at night.

Emergency beacons

A personal locator beacon (PLB) sends your location to rescuers by satellite. Register it, carry it outside your pack, and only activate it in a life-threatening emergency. Satellite messengers and newer phones with satellite emergency features work the same way. Find open sky to get a connection.

Wear and lay out bright colors. Spread out a space blanket shiny side up, an orange vest, or a tarp. Movement catches the eye: wave a bright cloth on a stick.
