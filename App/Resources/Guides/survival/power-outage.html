Do this now

  1. Run generators outside only, at least 20 ft (6 m) from the house, with the exhaust pointed away from doors, windows, and vents.
  2. Never run a generator, grill, camp stove, or gas heater inside your home, garage, basement, or shed, even with doors open.
  3. Use battery-powered carbon monoxide alarms. If one sounds, or anyone feels dizzy, sick, or very sleepy, get everyone outside and call 911.
  4. Use flashlights, not candles.
  5. Keep the fridge and freezer closed. See Food Safety Without Power.
  6. Unplug sensitive electronics to protect them from surges when power returns. Leave one light switched on so you know when power is back.

Carbon monoxide (CO) kills during outages

CO is invisible and has no smell. A generator makes as much CO as many cars. Signs of CO poisoning: headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain, and confusion. People who are asleep or drunk can die before they notice. If several people or pets feel sick at once, think CO. Go outside to fresh air immediately.

Generator safety

  • Never plug a generator into a wall outlet ("backfeeding"). It can kill utility workers and neighbors. Use heavy-duty outdoor extension cords plugged into the generator, or have an electrician install a transfer switch.
  • Keep it dry. Run it under an open canopy, not in the rain. Touch it only with dry hands.
  • Turn it off and let it cool before refueling. Store fuel outside in approved containers.

Staying safe at home

  • Don't use a gas oven or stove to heat your home.
  • Stay away from downed power lines. Report them.
  • Plan for medical devices that need power, like oxygen, CPAP, and power wheelchairs. Talk to your provider and utility before an emergency.
  • Keep phones charged with power banks, solar chargers, or a car charger. Run the car only outdoors, never in a garage.
  • In heat, go to a cooling center. In cold, see Winter Storms.

When the power comes back

  • Turn on appliances one at a time to avoid overload.
  • Check food and medicine before using them.
