Do this now

  1. DROP to your hands and knees.
  2. COVER your head and neck with one arm. Crawl under a sturdy table or desk if one is close. If not, crawl next to an inside wall, away from windows.
  3. HOLD ON to your shelter (or your head and neck) until the shaking stops.
  4. Don't run outside during shaking. Don't stand in a doorway.
  5. If in bed, stay there. Lie face down and cover your head and neck with a pillow.
  6. When it stops, check for injuries and hazards, then get out if the building is damaged.

Where you are

  • Outdoors: move away from buildings, trees, streetlights, and power lines, then drop, cover, and hold on.
  • Driving: pull over away from bridges, overpasses, and power lines. Stay in the car with your seat belt on until shaking stops.
  • Using a wheelchair or walker: lock your wheels, bend forward, and cover your head and neck with your arms or a pillow.

After the shaking

  • Expect aftershocks. Drop, cover, and hold on each time.
  • If you smell gas or hear a hissing sound, leave right away and turn off the gas at the meter only if you know how. Don't use lights, matches, or switches. Only the gas company should turn it back on.
  • Wear sturdy shoes. Broken glass is everywhere after a quake.
  • If trapped: cover your mouth with cloth. Tap on a pipe or wall or use a whistle so rescuers can hear you. Shout only as a last resort, since it uses energy and you can breathe in dust.
  • Don't enter damaged buildings.
Tsunami: if you are near the coast and feel a strong or long earthquake (about 20 seconds or more), or see the sea pull back suddenly, move to high ground or inland right away. Don't wait for a warning. Stay away until officials say it is safe. More waves often follow the first.
