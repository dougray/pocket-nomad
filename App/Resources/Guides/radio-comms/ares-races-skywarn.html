Do this now

  1. Want to help in disasters with radio? Get your ham license, then contact your county's ARES group.
  2. For government emergency communication, ask your local emergency management office about RACES.
  3. To report severe weather to forecasters, take a free SKYWARN storm spotter class from the National Weather Service. You don't need to be a ham.
  4. Join a weekly net to practice. Checking in builds your skills.

ARES (Amateur Radio Emergency Service)

  • Run by the ARRL, the national association for amateur radio.
  • Licensed hams who volunteer to provide radio communication for hospitals, the Red Cross, shelters, and emergency managers when normal systems are overloaded or down.
  • Groups are organized by county or region, with regular drills and nets.

RACES (Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service)

  • A special ham radio service set up under FCC rules for local, county, and state government emergency management.
  • Members are usually registered with a government agency and may need a background check.
  • Many hams belong to both ARES and RACES.

SKYWARN

  • A National Weather Service program of trained volunteer storm spotters.
  • Spotters report hail, high winds, flooding, and tornadoes. Their reports help forecasters issue warnings.
  • Training is free. Classes are offered by local NWS offices, in person and online.
  • Many spotters are hams, but anyone can take part. Reports can also go by phone or online.

Other groups

  • CERT (Community Emergency Response Team): free local training in disaster first aid, light search and rescue, and team organization. Often includes radio use.
  • National Traffic System (NTS): ham operators who pass formal written messages across the country.
Don't self-deploy in a disaster. Work through your group so you get assignments, safety briefings, and a place in the plan.
