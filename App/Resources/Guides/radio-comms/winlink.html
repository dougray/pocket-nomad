Do this now

  1. Get at least a Technician ham license. Winlink needs a call sign.
  2. Install a Winlink client on your computer. Pat is a free, open-source client for macOS, Linux, and Windows. Winlink Express is the official Windows client.
  3. Create your Winlink account with your call sign. Do this while you still have internet.
  4. Find nearby gateway stations (RMS) in the client's list for your band and mode.
  5. Send a test message to yourself. Practice before you need it.

What Winlink is

Winlink is a worldwide system that sends email over radio. Your radio connects to a gateway station, which passes your message onto the internet. If the internet is down in your area, a gateway far away can still deliver it. Hospitals, shelters, and emergency managers use it when normal email fails.

How it connects

PathRangeNotes
VHF/UHF packetLocal, tens of milesSlow but simple. Works with many 2 m radios and a computer sound card interface.
HFRegional to worldwideReaches distant gateways when local internet is out. Needs a General license for most HF use.
Peer-to-peerVariesStation to station directly, no internet needed at all.
TelnetInternetNormal internet link. Good for practice and setup.

Things to know

  • Messages are not private. Ham rules forbid encryption, and Winlink messages can be reviewed.
  • No business use. Ham rules apply.
  • Keep messages short and plain text. Attachments are slow over radio.
  • Winlink has standard emergency forms (like ICS-213 general messages) that agencies expect.
  • Winlink is run by volunteers through the Amateur Radio Safety Foundation and is free for licensed hams.
Join a local Winlink practice net. Many ARES groups hold weekly Winlink check-ins.
