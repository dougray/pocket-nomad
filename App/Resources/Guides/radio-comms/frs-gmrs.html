Do this now

  1. Turn the radio on and set it to the channel your group agreed on. If you have no plan, try channel 20, then channel 1.
  2. Turn privacy codes (CTCSS/DCS tones) off when calling for help, so you can hear everyone on the channel.
  3. Listen, then press and hold talk. Wait 1 second and speak.
  4. In a real emergency, say "Emergency" three times, then your location and what you need.
  5. Get high and away from buildings for better range.
Frequencies, nets, and rules here are for the United States. Other countries use different rules. Nets and local channel plans change over time, so confirm them with local radio groups when you can.

FRS vs. GMRS

FRS (Family Radio Service)GMRS (General Mobile Radio Service)
LicenseNoneRequired, no exam. One license covers your immediate family. Lasts 10 years. Apply at fcc.gov.
PowerUp to 2 W (0.5 W on ch 8 to 14)Up to 50 W on ch 15 to 22 with mobile or base radios
AntennaBuilt-in, can't be changedCan use better and external antennas
RepeatersNoYes. Repeaters greatly extend range.
Typical rangeUp to about 1 to 2 miles in most placesSeveral miles radio-to-radio, much farther via repeaters

FRS and GMRS share the same 22 channels, so FRS and GMRS radios can talk to each other.

GMRS repeaters

  • A repeater listens on one frequency and re-sends your voice on another from a high antenna.
  • Repeater inputs are 5 MHz higher than the outputs above (for example, channel 20R listens on 467.675 MHz and transmits on 462.675 MHz).
  • Most repeaters need a specific tone. Ask the owner. Many are listed online by the owners.

Rules to know

  • GMRS users must say their call sign at least every 15 minutes during a conversation and at the end.
  • No business use on FRS or GMRS by default. No profanity.
  • "Privacy codes" don't make your talk private. Anyone can hear you. They only filter out other users from your speaker.
Channel 20 (462.675 MHz) is widely used for emergencies and traveler help. Some groups add a 141.3 Hz tone on it. If you are monitoring for emergencies, leave tones off so you hear everything.
