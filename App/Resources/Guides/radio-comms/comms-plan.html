Do this now

  1. Pick one out-of-area contact everyone will text or call. Long-distance calls and texts often work when local ones don't.
  2. Pick two meeting places: one near home, one outside your neighborhood.
  3. Pick radio channels: a primary and a backup. Write them on a card for each person.
  4. Pick check-in times. For example: turn radios on at the top of every hour for 5 minutes (like 9:00 to 9:05) to save batteries.
  5. Practice once. Give everyone a copy of the card for their wallet, bag, and car.

Why a plan

In a disaster, phones may be down, people may be apart, and radios only work if everyone is on the same channel at the same time. A simple plan made now makes it far easier to find each other later.

The channels in this example are only an example. Choose your own and avoid the busiest local channels.

Tips

  • Text instead of calling. Texts use less network capacity and often get through when calls fail.
  • Keep radios off between check-ins to save power, and carry spare batteries.
  • Leave a note at home with the date, time, where you went, and who is with you.
  • Kids should know the plan, the out-of-area number by heart, and how to use the radio.
  • Include neighbors. Agree on a neighborhood channel.
  • Write the plan on paper. Phones die.
