Do this now

  1. Locally, first try your area's ham repeaters. They reach farthest. Look them up before an emergency and write them on your channel card.
  2. For direct radio-to-radio contact, try 146.520 MHz FM (2 m national simplex calling frequency).
  3. Then try 446.000 MHz FM (70 cm calling frequency).
  4. Listen on 162.400 to 162.550 MHz for NOAA weather alerts.
  5. Remember: the calling frequency is for making contact. Once you reach someone, move to another simplex frequency.
Frequencies, nets, and rules here are for the United States. Other countries use different rules. Nets and local channel plans change over time, so confirm them with local radio groups when you can.

HF voice nets (General license or higher)

Nets change their frequencies, schedules, and names. Treat these as starting points to listen. Tune nearby if you don't hear anything. Below 10 MHz, voice uses lower sideband (LSB). Above 10 MHz, upper sideband (USB).
