Do this now

  1. Wash your hands with soap often. This is the most important step.
  2. Take a quick wash once a day, even without a shower. Wipe face, armpits, groin, and feet with a soapy cloth, then a clean wet cloth.
  3. Brush your teeth with safe water. Use bottled, boiled, or treated water.
  4. Change into dry socks and underwear daily if you can.
  5. Keep cuts covered and clean.

Bathing with little water

  • A "sponge bath" uses about 1 to 2 quarts (1 to 2 L).
  • Baby wipes and no-rinse body wash help when water is short.
  • Water for washing should be safe enough not to make you sick if it gets in your mouth. Treat it with a little bleach if unsure.
  • Don't bathe in flood water.

Feet

  • Wet feet for long periods can cause painful skin damage (immersion foot or trench foot).
  • Dry your feet and change socks at least once a day. Let feet air out at night.
  • Check feet for blisters, sores, and cuts. Treat them early.

Teeth

  • Brush twice a day. If you have no toothbrush, rub teeth with a clean cloth.
  • Rinse with safe water only.

Shelters and crowded spaces

  • Cough and sneeze into your elbow.
  • Don't share towels, toothbrushes, cups, or razors.
  • Wear a mask if you are sick, and keep space from others.
  • Report diarrhea, vomiting, rash, or fever to shelter staff.
Clean clothes help prevent rashes and lice. If you can't wash clothes, air them out in the sun. Sunlight helps kill some germs and dries them.
