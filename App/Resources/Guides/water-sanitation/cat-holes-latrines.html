Do this now

  1. Pick a spot at least 200 ft (60 m) from any water, camp, trail, or food area. That is about 70 adult steps. Choose a spot downhill from water sources if possible.
  2. Cat hole (one use): dig 6 to 8 in (15 to 20 cm) deep and 4 to 6 in (10 to 15 cm) wide.
  3. Use it, then fill it back in with the dirt you dug out. Cover with leaves or a stone.
  4. For a group staying more than a day or two, dig a latrine trench instead.
  5. Wash hands right after.

Cat holes

  • Use rich, dark soil if you can. Germs break down faster there than in sand.
  • Spread cat holes out. Don't reuse the same spot.
  • Pack out toilet paper in a sealed bag if you can, or bury it deep in the hole.
  • Don't use cat holes in snow, on frozen ground, in narrow canyons, or near a dry streambed that could flood.

Trench latrine (groups)

  1. Choose a site at least 200 ft (60 m) from water and downhill from camp and wells. Keep it away from where people sleep and cook, but close enough to use at night.
  2. Dig a trench about 1 ft (30 cm) wide and 2 to 3 ft (60 to 90 cm) deep. Make it long enough for the group.
  3. Pile the dirt next to it with a shovel or can. Each person covers their waste with dirt after use.
  4. Build a screen for privacy. Add a handwashing station next to it.
  5. When waste is about 1 ft (30 cm) from the top, fill it in with dirt, mound it, and mark the spot. Dig a new trench.
Keep the bottom of any pit well above the water table. If water seeps into the hole, choose a higher spot. Don't dig latrines in flood zones.

Urine

Pee away from water, trails, and camps, on rocks or bare ground when possible.
