Do this now

  1. Bag garbage tightly and keep it in cans with tight lids.
  2. Store garbage outside, away from where people sleep, cook, and get water.
  3. Keep food waste sealed. It draws rats, flies, and other pests.
  4. Separate hazardous waste: chemicals, batteries, fuel, medicine, and broken electronics.
  5. Don't burn plastic, treated wood, or household trash near people.

Why it matters

Piles of trash attract rats, flies, and mosquitoes. These spread disease. Rotting food also brings bears and other animals in some areas.

Handling garbage

  • Wear gloves and sturdy shoes when handling debris and trash.
  • Bag food waste and diapers separately and tie them shut.
  • Drain standing water from containers, tires, and tarps to cut down on mosquitoes.
  • Follow local pickup rules after a disaster. Debris often must be sorted into piles (vegetation, construction, appliances, electronics, hazardous waste).

Hazardous waste

  • Keep it in its original container and away from children.
  • Don't pour fuel, oil, paint, or chemicals on the ground or down drains.
  • Keep car batteries and lithium batteries dry and apart. Damaged lithium batteries can catch fire.
  • Used needles go in a hard plastic bottle with a screw cap, labeled "sharps."

Burning

Only burn trash if local rules allow it and there is no fire ban. Burn only paper, cardboard, and untreated wood, far from buildings and dry brush. Never burn plastic, foam, rubber, or treated wood. They release toxic smoke.

Dead animals

Don't touch them with bare hands. Use gloves, a shovel, or double plastic bags. Contact local animal control or health officials for large animals.
