Do this now

  1. Wet your hands with clean water. Turn off the tap or stop the pour.
  2. Lather with soap. Scrub backs of hands, between fingers, and under nails for 20 seconds (hum "Happy Birthday" twice).
  3. Rinse under clean, flowing water. Don't rinse in a shared bowl.
  4. Dry with a clean towel or air dry.
  5. Wash before preparing food or eating, after the toilet, after diaper changes, after caring for the sick, and after touching garbage or flood water.

Build a tippy tap

A tippy tap is a foot-operated handwashing station. It uses very little water and you don't touch anything with dirty hands.

  1. Take a clean plastic jug with a handle (about 1 gallon or 4 L). Make a small hole near the top on the side opposite the handle, about the size of a nail.
  2. Run a string or stick through the handle so the jug can tip.
  3. Hang the jug from a crossbar between two posts, about chest high.
  4. Tie a string from the jug's neck down to a stick on the ground (the foot pedal).
  5. Fill the jug with water. Step on the pedal. The jug tips and pours a thin stream from the hole.
  6. Hang soap on a string from the crossbar. Put gravel under it so the ground doesn't get muddy.

Other simple setups

  • A water jug with a spigot set on a table, plus a bucket underneath to catch used water.
  • One person pours from a cup while another washes.

When there is no soap

  • Hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol works for many germs. Rub it all over your hands until dry.
  • Sanitizer doesn't work well on visibly dirty or greasy hands, and doesn't kill some germs, such as norovirus and Cryptosporidium. Use soap and water when you can.
  • If you have no soap or sanitizer, scrubbing with water alone, or with clean ash or sand, is better than nothing.
Water for handwashing should be clean, but it doesn't need to be as clean as drinking water. Water with a small amount of bleach added is fine.
