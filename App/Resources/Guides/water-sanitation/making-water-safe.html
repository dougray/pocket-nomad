Do this now

  1. Use bottled water first if you have it.
  2. If water is cloudy, let it settle, then pour it through a clean cloth, paper towel, or coffee filter.
  3. Boil it: bring water to a full rolling boil for 1 minute. Above 5,000 ft (about 1,500 m), boil for 3 minutes. Let it cool.
  4. If you can't boil, use plain, unscented household bleach (see the table below). Stir and wait 30 minutes.
  5. Store treated water in clean, covered containers.
Boiling is the surest way to kill germs. But no home method removes fuel, chemicals, or toxic metals. If water may be polluted with chemicals, oil, or fuel (for example after a chemical spill or flood near industry), find another water source.

Bleach dosing

Use regular, unscented liquid chlorine bleach. Check the label: it should say 6% or 8.25% sodium hypochlorite and be safe for disinfecting. Don't use scented, color-safe, splash-less, or "with cleaners" bleach. Bleach weakens over time. Use a fresh bottle if you can.

  • Settle and filter cloudy water first. If it is still cloudy, or is colored or very cold, double the amount.
  • Stir and wait at least 30 minutes.
  • The water should have a slight bleach smell. If it doesn't, add the same dose again and wait another 15 minutes.
  • If it tastes too strong, let it stand open for a few hours or pour it between two clean containers.
  • Use a clean medicine dropper. A standard drop is about 1/20 of a milliliter.

Iodine

  • 2% tincture of iodine from a first aid kit: add 5 drops per quart or liter of clear water, or 10 drops if cloudy. Wait 30 minutes.
  • Don't use iodine if you are pregnant, have thyroid problems, or are allergic to iodine. It is not meant for use over long periods.

Disinfection tablets

Chlorine dioxide, chlorine, or iodine tablets made for drinking water work well. Follow the package exactly. Wait times vary, sometimes up to 4 hours for some germs.

Filters

  • A good portable filter with a pore size of 1 micron or smaller removes most parasites (like Giardia and Cryptosporidium) and most bacteria.
  • Most filters do not remove viruses. After filtering, also boil or use chemicals, unless the filter is rated for viruses (a "purifier").
  • Filters do not remove salt, chemicals, or fuel. Activated carbon can reduce some chemicals and bad taste, but don't count on it.
  • Clean or replace the filter as the maker says. A clogged or cracked filter may let germs through.
Cryptosporidium is not reliably killed by bleach or iodine. Boiling, or a 1 micron filter followed by chemical treatment, gives better protection.

Solar disinfection (SODIS)

A last-resort method when you have nothing else. Fill clear plastic (PET) bottles of 2 liters or less with clear water. Lay them on their side in full sun for at least 6 hours, or 2 days if the sky is more than half cloudy. It doesn't work with cloudy water or in rain.

What not to do

  • Don't drink seawater or urine. They make dehydration worse.
  • Don't use pool or spa chemicals unless you know exactly how to dose them for drinking water.
  • Don't drink water from car radiators or heating systems. It contains antifreeze and other chemicals.
