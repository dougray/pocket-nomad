Do this now

  1. Pack menstrual supplies and baby supplies in every emergency kit.
  2. Keep breastfeeding if you are. Breast milk is safe, clean, and protects babies from illness.
  3. For formula, use ready-to-feed formula first. For powder, mix it only with bottled water or water that was boiled and cooled.
  4. Wash hands before feeding the baby and after diaper changes.
  5. Change pads, tampons, or cups regularly, and wash hands before and after.

Menstrual hygiene

  • Pads are easy to use in a disaster. Wrap used ones in paper or a bag and put them in a lidded bin.
  • Tampons: change every 4 to 8 hours as the package directs. Never leave one in longer than 8 hours (risk of toxic shock syndrome). Wash hands before inserting.
  • Menstrual cups: empty and rinse with safe water. Boil the cup between periods if you can.
  • Cloth pads: soak in cold water, wash with soap, and dry in the sun if possible. Change often.
  • Don't flush pads or tampons, especially when the sewer may be damaged.
  • Bring extra underwear, a small bag, and wipes. Ask shelter staff for supplies. Most shelters keep them.

Safe infant feeding

  • Breastfeeding is the safest choice in a disaster. Stress doesn't usually stop milk. Feeding often helps keep the supply up.
  • Ready-to-feed liquid formula needs no water. Keep a supply in your kit.
  • For powdered formula: use bottled water if you can. If not, boil water for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft) and let it cool. Don't use water treated with bleach or tablets for formula unless there is no other choice.
  • Throw out any formula left in the bottle after a feeding. Prepared formula left out more than 2 hours should be thrown out.
  • Use a clean cup if you can't clean bottles well. Cups are easier to clean.
  • Clean bottles and nipples with hot soapy water and safe rinse water. Boil them if you can.
Never water down formula to make it last. It can make a baby very sick. Ask shelters or relief workers for help.

Diapers

  • Change diapers on a clean surface, away from food areas.
  • Bag and seal dirty diapers. Wash hands right after.
  • Use wipes or a cloth with safe water to clean the baby.
  • Diaper rash gets worse when changes are delayed. Let skin air dry.
