Do this now

  1. Make sure the scene is safe. Tap and shout. If the person does not respond and is not breathing, or is only gasping, start CPR now.
  2. Call 911 on speaker. Send someone for an AED (automated external defibrillator).
  3. Put the person on their back on a firm surface.
  4. Adult: heel of one hand in the center of the chest, other hand on top. Lock your elbows. Push straight down at least 2 in (5 cm), but not more than about 2.4 in (6 cm).
  5. Push fast: 100 to 120 pushes per minute. That is the beat of the song "Stayin' Alive." Let the chest come all the way back up after each push.
  6. If you are trained and willing, give 30 pushes, then 2 breaths. If not, just keep pushing (hands-only CPR).
  7. As soon as the AED arrives, turn it on and do what it says.
  8. Do not stop until the person starts breathing, an AED tells you to stop, help takes over, or you are too tired to go on.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.
Gasping is not normal breathing. If a person is unresponsive and only gasping, start CPR.

Hands-only CPR (adults and teens)

If you see an adult or teen suddenly collapse, hands-only CPR works well for the first few minutes. Just push hard and fast in the center of the chest. You do not need to give breaths. Hands-only CPR is far better than doing nothing.

How to give rescue breaths

  1. Tilt the head back and lift the chin to open the airway.
  2. Pinch the nose shut. Seal your mouth over theirs. Use a CPR face shield if you have one.
  3. Blow for about 1 second, just enough to make the chest rise.
  4. Let the chest fall, then give the second breath.
  5. Go right back to chest pushes. Keep pauses under 10 seconds.

Breaths matter most for children, infants, drowning victims, drug overdoses, and anyone whose heart stopped because they could not breathe.

  • If you are alone with a child or infant and did not see them collapse: give about 2 minutes of CPR first (about 5 rounds of 30:2), then call 911.
  • Infant breaths: cover the baby's mouth and nose with your mouth. Use small puffs, only enough to make the chest rise. Tilt the head back only a little, to a neutral "sniffing" position.
  • Trained rescuers working in pairs on a child or infant use 15 pushes to 2 breaths.

Using an AED

  1. Turn it on. It talks to you. Follow the voice.
  2. Bare the chest. Wipe it dry if wet. Remove medicine patches with a gloved hand.
  3. Put on the pads as shown in the pictures on the pads.
  4. Do not touch the person while the AED checks the heart or gives a shock. Say "Clear!" loudly.
  5. After a shock (or if no shock is advised), start pushes again right away.
  6. For a child under 8 or under about 55 lb (25 kg), use child pads if you have them. If not, adult pads are OK. Make sure the pads don't touch each other. You may need to put one on the chest and one on the back.

Tips

  • Push hard. You may feel ribs crack. Keep going. A broken rib heals. A stopped heart does not.
  • If others are there, switch every 2 minutes so the pushes stay strong.
  • If the person vomits, roll them to the side, wipe out the mouth, roll them back, and keep going.
  • If the person starts breathing normally, put them in the recovery position and watch them closely.
