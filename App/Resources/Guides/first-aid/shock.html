Do this now

  1. Call 911. Shock can kill even when the injury looks small.
  2. Stop any heavy bleeding first.
  3. Have the person lie flat on their back.
  4. Keep them warm. Put a blanket or coat over them and something under them if the ground is cold.
  5. Do not give food or drink, even if they ask. They may need surgery, and they may vomit.
  6. Talk calmly. Keep checking breathing. If breathing stops, start CPR.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

What shock is

Shock means the body is not getting enough blood flow. It is often caused by heavy bleeding (inside or outside), severe burns, a bad allergic reaction, serious infection, dehydration, or heart problems. It gets worse over time.

Signs of shock

  • Pale, gray, or bluish skin. Cold and clammy (sweaty) skin.
  • Fast, weak pulse. Fast, shallow breathing.
  • Restless, anxious, confused, or very sleepy.
  • Very thirsty. Feeling sick to the stomach or vomiting.
  • Dizzy or fainting.

Care

  • If they are vomiting or not fully awake, turn them on their side (unless you suspect a spine injury).
  • If they have trouble breathing, they may be more comfortable sitting partly up.
  • Do not raise the head.
  • Loosen tight clothing.
  • Write down what you see and what time it happened. Give this to responders.
Some courses teach raising the legs about 12 in (30 cm) if there is no injury to the head, neck, back, hips, or legs. Only do this if you were trained to and it does not cause pain. Lying flat and keeping warm are the main steps.
