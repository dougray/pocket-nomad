Do this now

  1. Call 911. Put on gloves if you have them.
  2. Find where the blood is coming from. Cut or pull away clothing if needed.
  3. Press hard on the wound with a clean cloth, gauze, or your hand. Push with both hands and lean your weight on it.
  4. Do not lift the cloth to look. If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top and keep pressing.
  5. If blood is spurting, pooling, or won't stop on an arm or leg, use a tourniquet 2 to 3 in (5 to 8 cm) above the wound. Never put it on a joint.
  6. Tighten the tourniquet until the bleeding stops. Write down the time. Do not loosen it.
  7. For deep wounds in the groin, armpit, or neck, pack the wound tightly with gauze or cloth, then press hard for at least 3 minutes.
  8. Keep the person lying down and warm. Watch for shock.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.
Life-threatening bleeding: blood spurting out, blood pooling on the ground, clothes soaked with blood, a lost arm or leg, or bleeding that won't stop with pressure. A person can bleed to death in a few minutes. Act fast.

Direct pressure

Pressing hard is the first thing to try for almost any bleeding. Push straight down on the wound, not near it. Keep pressing until help takes over or the bleeding stops. This can take 10 minutes or more.

Tourniquet (arms and legs only)

  1. Place the band 2 to 3 in (5 to 8 cm) above the wound, between the wound and the heart. If you can't see the wound clearly, go high and tight, near the top of the arm or leg.
  2. Never place it over the elbow or knee. If the wound is right below a joint, go above the joint.
  3. Pull the strap as tight as you can, then twist the rod (windlass) until the bleeding stops.
  4. Lock the rod in place.
  5. Write the time on the tourniquet or on the person's forehead (for example, "TQ 14:32").
  6. If one tourniquet doesn't stop the bleeding, put a second one just above the first.
  • It will hurt. A tourniquet that works is very painful. That is normal. Do not loosen it.
  • Do not remove it. Only medical staff should take off a tourniquet.
  • A commercial tourniquet (like a CAT or SOF-T) works best. Store one in each kit and practice with it.
  • Improvised tourniquet: use a strip of cloth at least 2 in (5 cm) wide. Narrow cords and wires cut the skin and don't work well. Tie it around the limb, put a stick in the knot, and twist until the bleeding stops. Then tie the stick down. Improvised tourniquets often fail, so keep watching.

Wound packing

Use packing for deep wounds where a tourniquet can't go: groin, armpit, neck, buttocks, or a large gaping wound on a limb.

  1. Push gauze (hemostatic gauze if you have it, or any clean cloth) deep into the wound, right onto the bleeding spot.
  2. Keep stuffing until the wound is full and you can't fit any more.
  3. Press hard on top with both hands for at least 3 minutes with hemostatic gauze, and longer, up to 10 minutes, with plain gauze.
  4. Wrap a pressure bandage tightly over it, or keep pressing.
Do not pack wounds of the chest or belly. Press on them instead, and get help fast. For a chest wound with bubbling or sucking air, use a vented chest seal if you have one. If not, leave it open or cover it loosely with clean, dry gauze or cloth. Don't seal it airtight with plastic or tape. If breathing gets worse, loosen or remove the cover.

Other tips

  • If something is stuck in the wound (knife, stick, glass), leave it in. Pad around it and press around it, not on it.
  • Lay the person down. Cover them to keep them warm. Bleeding people get cold fast, and cold makes bleeding worse.
  • For an amputated part: wrap it in clean, damp gauze, put it in a plastic bag, and put the bag on ice. Don't let the part touch ice directly. Send it with the person.
  • Wash your hands well afterward.
