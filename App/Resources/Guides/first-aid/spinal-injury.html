Do this now

  1. Call 911.
  2. Tell the person not to move. Do not move them unless the scene is dangerous (fire, traffic, water) or you must turn them to do CPR or clear vomit.
  3. Kneel at their head. Place your hands on both sides of the head to keep it still, in the position you found it.
  4. If they are breathing, keep holding the head still until help arrives.
  5. If they stop breathing, start CPR. Breathing comes before protecting the spine.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

When to suspect a neck or back injury

  • A fall from higher than standing height, or a diving accident.
  • A car, motorcycle, or bike crash, or being hit by a car.
  • A hard blow to the head, neck, or back.
  • The person has neck or back pain, numbness, tingling, or weakness in arms or legs.
  • The person is confused, drunk, or not awake after an injury. They can't tell you what hurts.

If you must move them

  • Log roll to turn someone who is vomiting: with helpers, turn the head, shoulders, and hips together as one unit, like a log. One person holds the head and calls the count.
  • To drag someone out of danger, pull them by the clothes at the shoulders, along the length of the body. Support the head between your forearms. Don't drag them sideways.
Do not try to straighten the neck if it hurts or meets resistance. Do not take off a motorcycle or sports helmet unless you must to open the airway.

In the water

If someone may have hurt their neck in shallow water, keep them floating face up with their head in line with their body. Hold them still in the water until help arrives, unless you must get them out to give CPR.
