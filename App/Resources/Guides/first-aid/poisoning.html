Do this now

  1. If the person is not awake, having a seizure, or having trouble breathing, call 911.
  2. If they are awake and OK, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 (US). It is free, private, and open 24/7.
  3. Get them away from the poison. Move them to fresh air for gas or fumes, if it is safe for you.
  4. Don't make them vomit unless Poison Control tells you to.
  5. Keep the container or label to show responders or read to Poison Control.
  6. For poison on the skin, remove clothing and rinse the skin with running water for 15 to 20 minutes. For eyes, see Eye Injuries.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

What to tell Poison Control or 911

  • The person's age and weight.
  • What they took or touched, how much, and when.
  • How they are acting now.

Carbon monoxide (CO)

CO is a gas you can't see or smell. It comes from generators, grills, camp stoves, cars, and fuel heaters. Signs: headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, confusion, and sleepiness. Several people or pets sick at the same time is a strong clue. Get everyone outside to fresh air right away and call 911. Don't go back in. See Power Outage Safety.

Other common poisonings

  • Medicine overdose: call Poison Control even if the person feels fine. Some overdoses (like acetaminophen) cause damage that shows up later.
  • Opioid overdose (very slow or no breathing, blue lips, tiny pupils, won't wake up): call 911. Give naloxone (Narcan) nasal spray if you have it. Start CPR if they are not breathing normally.
  • Button batteries: if a child may have swallowed one, go to an emergency room right away. They can burn through the throat in hours.
  • Cleaning chemicals: don't give anything to "neutralize" them. Call Poison Control.
  • Mushrooms or wild plants: save a sample for identification and call Poison Control.
Never mix bleach with ammonia, vinegar, or other cleaners. It makes toxic gas.
