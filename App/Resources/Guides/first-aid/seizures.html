Do this now

  1. Stay calm. Note the time the seizure started.
  2. Move hard or sharp objects away. Ease the person to the floor if they are standing or sitting.
  3. Put something soft and flat under their head. Loosen anything tight around the neck.
  4. Do not hold them down. Do not put anything in their mouth. They cannot swallow their tongue.
  5. When the jerking stops, roll them onto their side to keep the airway clear.
  6. Stay with them until they are fully awake. Speak calmly. They may be confused for a while.
  7. Call 911 if the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, or for any reason in the list below.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Call 911 if

  • The seizure lasts more than 5 minutes.
  • Another seizure starts soon after the first one, or they don't wake up between seizures.
  • It is their first seizure, or you don't know if they have a seizure disorder.
  • They have trouble breathing, or are not back to normal 5 to 10 minutes after the seizure stops.
  • A baby younger than 6 months has a seizure.
  • They got hurt, the seizure happened in water, or they are pregnant or have diabetes.

Other types of seizures

Not all seizures cause jerking. Some people stare blankly, pick at their clothes, or wander and seem unaware. Gently guide them away from danger. Don't grab them or try to snap them out of it.

Babies and young children with fever

Young children can have a seizure from a high fever. Follow the same steps. Remove extra clothing to help cool them, but don't put them in a cold bath. Have a doctor check them afterward. Call 911 if the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes or they have trouble breathing.

If the person has a seizure action plan or rescue medicine prescribed by their doctor, follow that plan.
