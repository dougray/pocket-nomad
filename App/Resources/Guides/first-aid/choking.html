Do this now

  1. Ask, "Are you choking?" If they can cough hard or talk, let them keep coughing. Stay with them.
  2. If they cannot cough, talk, cry, or breathe, or are making high-pitched sounds, act now. Have someone call 911.
  3. Adult or child over 1: stand behind them. Give 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
  4. Then give 5 abdominal thrusts: fist just above the belly button, thumb side in, grab it with your other hand, and pull in and up hard.
  5. Keep switching 5 back blows and 5 thrusts until the object comes out, they can breathe, or they pass out.
  6. Infant under 1: use 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts. Never use abdominal thrusts on a baby.
  7. If the person passes out, lower them to the ground, call 911, and start CPR.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Adult or child (1 year and older)

  • Back blows: lean the person forward. Strike hard between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand. Each blow is a separate try to knock the object loose.
  • Abdominal thrusts: stand behind them. For a small child, kneel. Make a fist and put the thumb side just above the belly button, well below the breastbone. Grab your fist with your other hand. Pull sharply in and up.
  • The American Heart Association teaches abdominal thrusts alone. The Red Cross teaches back blows plus thrusts. Both work. Use the method you were trained in.

Pregnant or very large person

Put your fist in the center of the breastbone instead, and give chest thrusts straight back.

If you are alone and choking

  • Call 911 even if you can't talk. Dispatch may be able to find you.
  • Give yourself abdominal thrusts with your fist, or push your upper belly hard against the back of a chair or a counter edge.

Infant (under 1 year)

  1. Sit or kneel. Lay the baby face down along your forearm, with the head lower than the chest. Support the jaw with your hand. Don't cover the mouth.
  2. Give 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
  3. Support the head and turn the baby face up on your other forearm, head still lower than the chest.
  4. Put 2 fingers in the center of the chest, just below the nipple line. Give 5 quick chest thrusts about 1.5 in (4 cm) deep.
  5. Repeat until the object comes out, the baby cries or coughs hard, or the baby goes limp.

If the person becomes unresponsive

  1. Lower them gently to the floor. Call 911 if no one has yet.
  2. Start CPR with chest pushes. Pushes can force the object out.
  3. Each time you open the airway to give breaths, look in the mouth. If you see the object, take it out with a finger. Never do a blind finger sweep. It can push the object deeper.
Anyone who needed back blows or thrusts should be checked by a doctor afterward. Thrusts can cause internal injury.
