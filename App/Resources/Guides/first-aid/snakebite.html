Do this now

  1. Move away from the snake. Don't try to catch or kill it. A photo from a safe distance is fine.
  2. Call 911. Get to a hospital as fast as you safely can. Antivenom is the only real treatment.
  3. Keep the person calm and still. Carry them if you can, or have them walk slowly.
  4. Remove rings, watches, and tight clothing near the bite before swelling starts.
  5. Keep the bitten limb still and at about the level of the heart. Don't raise it high or let it hang down.
  6. Cover the bite loosely with a clean, dry bandage.
  7. Note the time of the bite. Mark the edge of the swelling with a pen every 15 to 30 minutes.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Do NOT

  • Cut the bite.
  • Try to suck out the venom, by mouth or with a suction device.
  • Use a tourniquet or tight band.
  • Wrap it tightly with an elastic or pressure bandage. (Pressure wraps used for some snakes in Australia are not for US snakes.)
  • Put ice on it or soak it in water.
  • Give alcohol, caffeine, or pain medicine like aspirin or ibuprofen.
  • Use electric shock.

These do not help and can cause more harm.

Signs of a venomous bite

  • One or two puncture marks.
  • Pain, swelling, and bruising that spread from the bite.
  • Nausea, vomiting, sweating, or weakness.
  • Numbness or tingling, a metallic taste, or trouble breathing or swallowing.

Some bites don't inject venom ("dry bites"), but you can't tell right away. Treat every bite from an unknown or venomous snake as serious.

US venomous snakes

  • Pit vipers (rattlesnakes, copperheads, cottonmouths/water moccasins) cause most bites. They have a heavy body, a triangular head, and a pit between the eye and nostril.
  • Coral snakes have red, yellow, and black bands. Their bites may cause few signs at first but can affect breathing hours later. Go to the hospital even if the person feels fine.
A dead snake can still bite by reflex. Don't pick one up, even if it was killed.

Preventing bites

  • Wear boots and long pants in snake areas. Use a flashlight at night.
  • Don't put hands or feet where you can't see, like under rocks or logs.
  • Flood waters drive snakes into homes and debris. Be careful during cleanup.
