Do this now

  1. If the person is confused, shaky, sweaty, or acting strangely and has diabetes, assume low blood sugar.
  2. If they are awake and can swallow safely, give fast sugar: about 15 to 20 grams. Examples: glucose tablets or gel (follow label), 4 oz (120 mL) of juice or regular soda, or a tablespoon of sugar or honey.
  3. Wait 15 minutes. If they are not better, give the same amount again. Call 911 if still not better.
  4. Once they feel better, give a snack or meal.
  5. If they can't swallow safely, are passing out, or have a seizure: don't give anything by mouth. Call 911. Put them on their side. If they have glucagon and you know how to use it, give it.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Low blood sugar (hypoglycemia)

Comes on fast, often within minutes. Signs:

  • Shaky, sweaty, pale, or dizzy.
  • Hungry, irritable, anxious, or confused.
  • Fast heartbeat. Blurred vision. Headache.
  • Can look like someone who is drunk.
  • Severe: seizures or passing out.

Causes include too much insulin, skipped meals, more activity than usual, and alcohol.

High blood sugar (hyperglycemia)

Usually develops over hours to days. Signs: very thirsty, peeing a lot, tired, blurry vision. A fruity breath smell, vomiting, belly pain, and deep fast breathing are danger signs of a serious condition (diabetic ketoacidosis). Get medical care fast.

If you're not sure whether blood sugar is high or low and the person is awake and can swallow, give sugar. It helps a lot if sugar is low and will do little harm in the short term if it is high. If they have a meter, check.

Diabetes in a disaster

  • Keep at least a few weeks of supplies, plus fast sugar, in your kit if possible.
  • Insulin in use can usually be kept at room temperature for a limited time. Check the package insert for your insulin. Keep it out of heat and direct sun. Don't freeze it.
  • Stress, less food, and more physical work can all change blood sugar. Check more often.
