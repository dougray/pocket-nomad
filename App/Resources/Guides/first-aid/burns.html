Do this now

  1. Stop the burning. Get the person away from the heat. If clothes are on fire: stop, drop, and roll, or smother with a blanket.
  2. Cool the burn under cool running water for 20 minutes. Start as soon as you can. Cooling helps up to 3 hours after the burn.
  3. Take off rings, watches, belts, and tight clothing near the burn before it swells. Don't pull off anything stuck to the skin.
  4. Cover the burn loosely with plastic wrap or a clean, dry, non-fluffy cloth.
  5. Keep the rest of the person warm. Cool the burn, not the person.
  6. Call 911 for serious burns (see the list below).
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.
Do NOT use ice, ice water, butter, oil, toothpaste, or any home remedy. Don't pop blisters. Don't put fluffy cotton on a burn.

Call 911 or get medical care if

  • The burn is larger than the person's palm, or covers more than one body part.
  • It is on the face, head, neck, hands, feet, genitals, or over a major joint.
  • It is deep: white, leathery, brown, or black skin, or skin with no feeling.
  • It wraps all the way around an arm, leg, finger, or the chest.
  • The person has trouble breathing, a hoarse voice, burned nose hairs, or soot around the mouth. These signs mean the airway may be burned.
  • It was caused by chemicals, electricity, or an explosion.
  • The person is a young child, an older adult, or someone with a serious health problem.

Burn types

TypeWhat it looks like
Superficial (1st degree)Red, dry, painful, like a sunburn. No blisters.
Partial thickness (2nd degree)Red, blistered, wet-looking, very painful.
Full thickness (3rd degree)White, brown, black, or leathery. May not hurt because nerves are damaged. Always serious.

Special burns

  • Chemical: brush off dry powder first, then flush with lots of running water for at least 20 minutes. Remove contaminated clothing. Protect yourself from the chemical.
  • Electrical: turn off the power before you touch the person. Electrical burns can look small but cause deep damage and heart problems. Always get them checked. See the Electric Shock guide.
  • Eyes: see the Eye Injuries guide.

Minor burn care

  • After cooling, you can use a thin layer of aloe gel or plain petroleum jelly and a loose, non-stick bandage.
  • Over-the-counter pain medicine (like acetaminophen or ibuprofen) can help. Follow the label.
  • Watch for infection: more pain, redness, swelling, pus, or fever.
If there is no running water, use any clean, cool water. Pour it over the burn or soak a cloth and keep it wet and cool. Don't let a large burn area make the person shiver.
