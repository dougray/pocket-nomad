Do this now

  1. Move the person to a cool, shady place. Loosen or remove extra clothing.
  2. Is the person confused, slurring words, passing out, having a seizure, or very hot to the touch? That is heat stroke. Call 911 now.
  3. Heat stroke: cool them fast. Best: put them in cold water up to the neck. Next best: soak them with cold water and fan them, and put ice packs on the neck, armpits, and groin.
  4. Heat exhaustion: have them lie down, sip cool water or a sports drink, and use cool wet cloths.
  5. Get medical help if heat exhaustion is not better in 1 hour, they vomit, or they get worse.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Heat exhaustion vs. heat stroke

Heat exhaustionHeat stroke (emergency)
MindAwake, may be dizzy or weakConfused, slurred speech, strange behavior, seizures, or passes out
SkinCool, pale, clammy, heavy sweatingHot. May be red. May be dry or still sweating
Body tempNormal to raisedVery high, 103 °F (39.4 °C) or more
OtherHeadache, nausea, cramps, thirst, fast weak pulseFast strong pulse, headache, nausea
What to doCool down, rest, drinkCall 911. Cool immediately.
Heat stroke can cause death or brain damage. Cool first, then transport. Keep cooling until help arrives or the person is back to normal. Don't give drinks to someone who is confused or can't swallow.

Heat cramps

Painful muscle spasms during heavy sweating. Stop activity, rest in a cool place, drink water or a sports drink, and stretch gently.

Preventing heat illness

  • Drink water often, before you feel thirsty.
  • Rest in the shade. Do hard work in the early morning or evening.
  • Wear light, loose clothing and a hat.
  • Never leave a child, person, or pet in a parked car.
  • Check on older adults, babies, and people with health problems.
