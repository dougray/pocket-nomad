Do this now

  1. Stop and look. Before you go to the person, check for fire, traffic, downed power lines, deep or fast water, smoke, gas smell, unstable buildings, or violence.
  2. If the scene is not safe, do not go in. Call 911 and wait at a safe distance. You cannot help anyone if you get hurt too.
  3. Check the person: tap their shoulder and shout, "Are you OK?" Look for breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Look for heavy bleeding.
  4. Call 911, or point at one person and say, "You, call 911 and bring back an AED."
  5. Care: treat the worst problem first. No breathing means start CPR. Heavy bleeding means press hard on the wound.
  6. Stay with the person until help arrives. Keep checking breathing and how alert they are.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Check, Call, Care

This is the basic order for any emergency. It keeps you safe and makes sure help is on the way.

1. Check the scene, then the person

  • Ask: What happened? Is it still dangerous? How many people are hurt? Who can help me?
  • Wear gloves if you have them. Use a plastic bag over your hand if you don't.
  • If the person is awake, tell them your name and ask, "Can I help you?" If they say no, do not force care. Call 911.
  • If they are not awake, you can assume they would want help.
  • Do not move someone unless they are in danger where they are (fire, traffic, water) or you must move them to do CPR.

2. Call for help

  • Tell the dispatcher: where you are (address, cross streets, mile marker, or GPS coordinates), what happened, how many people are hurt, and what you are doing.
  • Do not hang up until the dispatcher tells you to.
  • If there is no phone signal, try texting 911. Many US areas accept 911 texts. A text may get through when a call won't.
  • If there is no way to call, send a runner, or use a radio (see the Radio and Comms guides).

3. Care for the most serious problem first

  1. Not breathing, or only gasping: start CPR.
  2. Heavy bleeding: press hard on it, or use a tourniquet on an arm or leg.
  3. Choking: back blows and thrusts.
  4. Then shock, burns, broken bones and smaller wounds.
Recovery position: If a person is breathing but not awake, and you don't think they have a neck or back injury, roll them onto their side with the top knee bent. This keeps the airway open and lets vomit drain out.

Good Samaritan laws

All US states have laws that protect people who give reasonable first aid in good faith. Do what you have been trained to do. Do not try things you have no training for.
