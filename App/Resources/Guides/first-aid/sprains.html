Do this now

  1. Stop using the injured joint. Rest it.
  2. Ice: put a cold pack or a bag of ice wrapped in a thin cloth on it for up to 20 minutes. Do this every 2 to 3 hours for the first day or two.
  3. Compress: wrap it with an elastic bandage. Snug, not tight.
  4. Elevate: raise it above the level of the heart if you can.
  5. If you can't put any weight on it, it looks bent, or it is numb, treat it as a broken bone and splint it.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Sprain or strain?

  • A sprain is a stretched or torn ligament (the bands that hold a joint together). Common in ankles, knees, and wrists.
  • A strain is a stretched or torn muscle or tendon.
  • Both cause pain, swelling, and bruising. Both get the same first aid.

Tips

  • Never put ice right on bare skin. It can cause frostbite.
  • No ice? Use a bag of frozen vegetables or a cloth soaked in cold water.
  • If the wrap makes fingers or toes tingle, go numb, turn blue, or feel cold, loosen it.
  • Over-the-counter pain medicine can help. Follow the label.
  • After a few days, start moving the joint gently as pain allows.

Get medical care if

  • You can't walk more than a few steps, or can't use the joint at all.
  • There is pain right over a bone.
  • The joint looks deformed or is unstable.
  • Numbness, or the skin turns pale or blue.
  • It does not get better within a few days.
If you must walk out on a sprained ankle, keep your boot on and laced. It acts like a splint. Taking it off may let the ankle swell so much that the boot won't go back on.
