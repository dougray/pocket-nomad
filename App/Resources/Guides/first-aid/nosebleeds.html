Do this now

  1. Have the person sit up and lean forward. Don't tip the head back. That makes them swallow blood.
  2. Pinch the soft part of the nose (just below the bony bridge) firmly with thumb and finger.
  3. Hold steady pressure for 10 to 15 minutes. Don't let go to check.
  4. Have them breathe through the mouth and spit out blood.
  5. If bleeding continues, pinch again for another 10 to 15 minutes. A cold pack on the bridge of the nose may help.
  6. Get medical care if it lasts more than 20 minutes, is very heavy, or followed a head injury.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Get emergency care if

  • Bleeding won't stop after about 20 minutes of firm pressure.
  • It follows a hit to the head or face, or the nose looks broken.
  • The person feels faint, weak, or short of breath.
  • They take blood thinners, or bleeding is very heavy.

After it stops

  • Don't blow or pick the nose for several hours.
  • Avoid bending over or heavy lifting for the rest of the day.
  • Dry air causes many nosebleeds. A little petroleum jelly inside the nostrils can help.
