Do this now

  1. If bleeding is heavy, see the Severe Bleeding guide first.
  2. Wash your hands. Put on gloves if you have them.
  3. Press with a clean cloth until small bleeding stops.
  4. Rinse the wound well with clean running water for several minutes. Use the cleanest water you have.
  5. Wash around the wound with soap. Try not to get soap deep in the wound.
  6. Remove dirt and small bits with tweezers cleaned with alcohol. Leave large or deep objects in place.
  7. Cover it with a clean bandage. You can use a thin layer of antibiotic ointment or petroleum jelly first.
  8. Check it and change the bandage every day, or when it gets wet or dirty.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Get medical care if

  • The wound is deep, gaping, or longer than about 1/2 in (1 cm) and may need stitches. Stitches work best when done within a few hours.
  • It is on the face, hands, or over a joint.
  • It was from an animal or human bite, a rusty or dirty object, or a puncture (like stepping on a nail).
  • You can't get it clean.
  • The person's tetanus shot is not up to date (see below).

Signs of infection

  • Redness that spreads, or red streaks going away from the wound.
  • More pain, swelling, or warmth after the first day or two.
  • Pus or a bad smell.
  • Fever or chills.

Red streaks, fever, or a person who feels very sick can mean the infection is spreading. Get medical care quickly.

Tetanus

Tetanus germs live in soil, dust, and animal waste. They can enter through any break in the skin. For a dirty or deep wound, adults need a tetanus booster if it has been more than 5 years since the last one. For clean, minor wounds, a booster is needed if it has been more than 10 years. Disaster cleanup causes many such wounds.

Closing wounds

  • Small, clean, straight cuts can be held together with adhesive strips (butterfly bandages).
  • Don't close dirty wounds, bites, or punctures. Closing them traps germs inside. Keep them clean and covered and get care.

Without clean supplies

  • Boiled and cooled water works for rinsing. Don't pour hydrogen peroxide or alcohol into the wound. They harm healthy tissue.
  • A clean cloth that has been boiled, or ironed, can be used as a bandage.
