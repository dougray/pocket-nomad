Do this now

  1. Keep the fridge and freezer doors closed.
  2. A closed fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours. A full closed freezer keeps food frozen about 48 hours (about 24 hours if half full).
  3. After 4 hours without power, move milk, meat, and leftovers into a cooler with ice.
  4. Throw out perishable food that has been above 40 °F (4 °C) for 2 hours or more.
  5. When in doubt, throw it out. Never taste food to see if it is safe.

Before the power goes out

  • Keep appliance thermometers in the fridge and freezer. The fridge should be at 40 °F (4 °C) or below. The freezer should be at 0 °F (-18 °C) or below.
  • Freeze containers of water and gel packs to keep things cold.
  • Group food together in the freezer. It stays cold longer.
  • Know where you can get dry ice or block ice.

After the power comes back

  • Freezer: food that still has ice crystals or is at 40 °F (4 °C) or below can be refrozen or cooked. It may lose some quality.
  • Fridge: throw out meat, poultry, seafood, milk, soft cheese, eggs, leftovers, cut fruit, and cooked foods that were above 40 °F (4 °C) for 2 hours or more.
  • Usually OK: hard cheese, butter, whole raw fruits and vegetables, jams, mustard, ketchup, peanut butter, and bread.
Food can look and smell fine and still make you sick. Germs that cause food poisoning often have no smell.

Flood water and food

  • Throw out all food that touched flood water, including anything in jars, boxes, plastic bags, or home-canned food.
  • Undamaged commercial cans and foil pouches can be saved: remove the labels, wash the cans, then sanitize them. Boil them in water for 2 minutes, or soak them for 15 minutes in a solution of 1 tablespoon of unscented bleach per gallon of clean water. Re-label with a marker.
  • Throw out cans that are bulging, leaking, rusted, or badly dented.
  • Throw out wooden cutting boards, baby bottle nipples, and pacifiers that touched flood water.

Cooking without power

Use grills, camp stoves, and generators outside only, far from doors and windows. Never use them in the house, garage, or a tent. Carbon monoxide can kill in minutes.

Medicines

Some medicines, like insulin, need to stay cool. If they were too warm for too long, ask a pharmacist or doctor. In a life-threatening situation, use the medicine until you can get a new supply.
