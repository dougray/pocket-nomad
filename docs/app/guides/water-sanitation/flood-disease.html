Do this now

  1. Stay out of flood water as much as you can. It often has sewage, chemicals, sharp debris, and live electrical wires.
  2. If you must go in, wear rubber boots, rubber gloves, and goggles.
  3. Wash any skin that touched flood water with soap and clean water as soon as you can.
  4. Keep cuts and wounds clean and covered. Get a tetanus shot if you are not up to date.
  5. Drink only bottled, boiled, or treated water until officials say tap water is safe.
  6. Wash hands often, especially before eating.

Main health risks after floods

  • Stomach illness from dirty water and food: diarrhea, vomiting, cramps. See Dehydration.
  • Wound infections: cuts that touch flood water can get badly infected. Watch for redness, swelling, pus, and fever.
  • Tetanus from deep or dirty wounds.
  • Carbon monoxide from generators and pumps used indoors. This kills people after nearly every flood.
  • Mold in wet buildings. See the Mold guide.
  • Mosquito-borne illness from standing water.
  • Electrocution from wet wiring and downed lines.
  • Drowning in moving water. Turn around, don't drown.

Kids and flood water

Don't let children play in flood water or with toys that touched it. Wash and disinfect toys with a bleach solution, or throw them out. Soft toys that can't be washed should be thrown out.

Wells

If your private well was flooded, don't drink from it until it has been disinfected and tested. Contact your local health department for testing.

Insects

  • Use insect repellent with an EPA-registered ingredient such as DEET or picaridin. Follow the label.
  • Wear long sleeves and pants at dawn and dusk.
  • Dump standing water from buckets, tires, and flower pots.
