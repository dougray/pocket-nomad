Do this now

  1. S.T.O.P. Stop. Think. Observe. Plan. Sit down, breathe slowly, and don't rush. Panic causes more harm than most dangers.
  2. Deal with immediate threats first: heavy bleeding, not breathing, fire, rising water, or extreme cold.
  3. Get shelter from cold, heat, wind, and rain.
  4. Get water. Make it safe to drink.
  5. Make it easy to be found: tell people your plan, signal, and stay put if lost.
  6. Food comes last. Most people can go weeks without it.

The rule of 3s

A rough guide to what kills fastest. Real limits vary with weather, health, and age.

You can survive aboutWithout
3 minutesAir (or with severe bleeding)
3 hoursShelter in harsh weather (cold, wet, wind, extreme heat)
3 daysWater
3 weeksFood

Use it to decide what to do first. Many people worry about food when they should be getting out of the wind.

Your mindset

  • Fear is normal. Name it, then take one small step.
  • Make a short plan, write it down if you can, and do the next task.
  • Rest when you can. Tired people make mistakes.
  • Keep a group together. Give everyone a job.
  • Save your phone battery: turn on Low Power Mode, lower the brightness, and use airplane mode when you don't need signal.

Take stock

  • Who is hurt? Give first aid.
  • What do you have? Lay out all gear, food, water, and clothing.
  • Who knows where you are? When will they expect you?
  • What is the weather doing? How much daylight is left?
The best survival tool is a plan made before you leave: tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back, and carry the basics (water, light, layers, a whistle, first aid kit, and a charged phone).
