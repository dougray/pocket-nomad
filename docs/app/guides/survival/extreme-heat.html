Do this now

  1. Stay in air conditioning if you can. If your home is hot, go to a cooling center, library, or mall during the hottest hours.
  2. Drink water often, even if you don't feel thirsty. Avoid alcohol and very sugary drinks.
  3. Stay out of the sun from late morning to late afternoon. Do outdoor work early or late.
  4. Wear light, loose, light-colored clothing and a wide hat.
  5. Take cool showers, or use wet cloths on your neck and wrists.
  6. Never leave children, older adults, or pets in a parked car.
  7. Check on neighbors, especially older adults and people who live alone.
Know the signs of heat stroke: confusion, slurred speech, passing out, seizures, or very hot skin. Call 911 and cool the person right away. See Heat Exhaustion vs. Heat Stroke.

Who is at highest risk

  • Babies and young children.
  • Adults over 65.
  • People with heart or lung disease, diabetes, obesity, or mental illness.
  • People on some medicines (ask a pharmacist).
  • Outdoor workers, athletes, and people without air conditioning or housing.

Keeping your home cooler

  • Close blinds and curtains on sunny windows during the day.
  • Open windows at night if it is cooler outside and safe to do so.
  • Stay on the lowest floor. Heat rises.
  • Use your oven and stove less.
  • Electric fans help, but in very high heat, fans alone may not prevent heat illness. Get to a cooled place.

Alerts

  • Heat Advisory: heat that can be dangerous for at-risk people.
  • Extreme Heat Warning (formerly Excessive Heat Warning): dangerous heat for everyone. Take action.
Pale yellow urine is a good sign you are drinking enough. Dark urine means drink more.
