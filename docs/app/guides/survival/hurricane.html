Do this now

  1. If told to evacuate, go. Storm surge (sea water pushed ashore) causes most hurricane deaths.
  2. If you stay, go to a small interior room, closet, or hallway on the lowest floor that is not at risk of flooding. Stay away from windows.
  3. Fill containers and a bathtub with water for washing and flushing.
  4. Charge phones and power banks. Get a battery or hand-crank radio.
  5. Don't go outside during the calm eye of the storm. The other side of the storm is coming.
  6. After the storm, watch out for flood water, downed lines, and carbon monoxide.

Before the storm

  • Know your evacuation zone and route. Plan where you will go: a friend's home inland, a hotel, or a shelter.
  • Bring in outdoor furniture and anything that could blow away.
  • Cover windows with storm shutters or plywood. Tape doesn't stop glass from breaking.
  • Fill your car's gas tank. Get cash. Refill medicines.
  • Take photos of your home and belongings for insurance.

Alerts

AlertMeaning
Hurricane WatchHurricane conditions are possible, usually within 48 hours
Hurricane WarningHurricane conditions are expected, usually within 36 hours
Storm Surge WarningLife-threatening flooding from rising sea water is expected
Don't wait out a hurricane in a mobile home or in a storm surge zone when told to leave. Flood water can rise faster than you can escape.

After

  • Listen for official news before going back.
  • Most hurricane deaths after the storm come from flooding, carbon monoxide, electrocution, heat, and injuries during cleanup.
  • Use chainsaws only if trained, and never alone.
  • See Power Outage Safety, Flood, and Food Safety Without Power.
