Do this now

  1. Turn around, don't drown. Never walk, swim, or drive through flood water.
  2. If told to evacuate, go right away. Move to higher ground.
  3. If water is rising around your home and you can't leave, go to the highest level. Go onto the roof only if you must. Don't go into a closed attic, where you could be trapped. Bring a tool to break out if you do.
  4. If your car is in rising water, get out and move to higher ground if you can do it safely. If water is rising inside the car, get onto the roof.
  5. Call 911 or signal for help. Wave a bright cloth or light.
  6. Stay away from downed power lines and any water near them.

Why flood water is so dangerous

  • 6 in (15 cm) of moving water can knock an adult down.
  • 1 ft (30 cm) of moving water can float many cars.
  • 2 ft (60 cm) of rushing water can sweep away most vehicles, including SUVs and pickups.
  • You can't see how deep it is or whether the road under it has washed away.
  • It is often full of sewage, chemicals, sharp debris, and snakes.

Flash floods

  • A flash flood can come within minutes of heavy rain, even if it isn't raining where you are.
  • Canyons, dry creek beds, low roads, and underpasses flood first.
  • If you hear a roar upstream, see water rising quickly, or see muddy water and debris, climb to high ground right away.

After the flood

  • Return home only when officials say it is safe.
  • Don't turn on power if you are standing in water, or if wiring got wet. Have it checked.
  • If you smell gas, leave and call the gas company from outside.
  • Throw out food that touched flood water. Drink only safe water. See the Water and Sanitation guides.
  • Take photos of damage for insurance before you clean.
Never run generators, pressure washers, or pumps with gas engines indoors or in a garage. See Power Outage Safety.
