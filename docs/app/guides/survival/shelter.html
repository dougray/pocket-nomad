Do this now

  1. Use what is already there: a car, building, cave entrance, rock overhang, or thick trees.
  2. Get off the ground. Put branches, leaves, a pad, or packs under you. The cold ground steals heat faster than air.
  3. Block the wind. Face the opening away from the wind.
  4. Stay dry. Put on rain gear or a trash bag before you get soaked.
  5. Keep it small. A small shelter is easier to warm with body heat.
  6. Build it before dark, while you still have energy.

Picking a site

  • Flat, dry, and a little higher than the ground around it.
  • Avoid: dry creek beds and low spots (flash floods), under dead trees or branches, at the base of cliffs or slopes (rockfall, avalanche), and hilltops in storms (lightning).
  • Near water, but not right next to it (cold air, bugs, and floods).
  • Where rescuers can see your signals.

Simple shelters

Tarp or poncho lean-to

  1. Tie a line between two trees about waist to chest high.
  2. Drape the tarp over it. Stake or weigh down the back edge on the side facing the wind.
  3. Pile leaves or branches on the sides to block the wind.

Debris hut

  1. Lean a long, strong pole against a stump or tree fork at about hip height. It should be a bit longer than you are tall.
  2. Lean sticks along both sides to form a ribbed tent shape just big enough to lie in.
  3. Pile leaves, pine needles, and brush over it, at least 2 to 3 ft (60 to 90 cm) thick. More is warmer.
  4. Fill the inside with a thick layer of dry leaves to lie on. Plug the door with a pile of leaves or your pack.

Your vehicle

A car is often the best shelter. It is visible and blocks wind. In snow, keep the exhaust pipe clear and don't run the engine for long. See Winter Storms.

Heat and desert

  • Get into shade during the day. Rest in the heat, move in the cool of morning and evening.
  • Shade 1 to 2 ft (30 to 60 cm) off the ground is cooler than ground-level shade. A double layer of cloth with an air gap helps.
  • Keep clothes on. They protect from sun and slow sweat loss.
In snow caves and snow shelters, always poke an air hole and keep it open. Never burn a stove or fire in a closed shelter. Carbon monoxide builds up fast.
