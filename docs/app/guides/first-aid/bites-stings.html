Do this now

  1. If there is any trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat, or faintness, treat it as anaphylaxis: use epinephrine and call 911.
  2. Move away from the insect, animal, or nest.
  3. Bee sting: scrape the stinger out sideways with a fingernail or card as soon as you can.
  4. Wash the area with soap and water.
  5. Put a cold pack wrapped in cloth on it for 10 to 20 minutes to ease pain and swelling.
  6. For any animal or human bite that breaks the skin, wash it with soap and running water for 15 minutes and get medical care.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Animal and human bites

  • Bites get infected easily. Wash well, cover, and see a health care provider.
  • Rabies: bites or scratches from bats, raccoons, skunks, foxes, coyotes, or any animal acting strangely can spread rabies. Rabies is almost always fatal once symptoms start, but shots given soon after the bite prevent it. Get care the same day.
  • If you wake up with a bat in the room, or find one near a child or a person who can't say if they were bitten, get medical advice. Bat bites can be too small to see.
  • Don't try to catch the animal. Note what it looked like and where it went.

Ticks

  1. Use fine-tipped tweezers. Grab the tick as close to the skin as you can.
  2. Pull straight up with steady, even pressure. Don't twist or jerk.
  3. Clean the bite and your hands with soap and water or rubbing alcohol.
  4. Don't use matches, nail polish, or petroleum jelly to make it let go.
  5. Watch for a rash (including a "bull's-eye" rash), fever, or aches over the next few weeks. See a provider if they appear.

Spiders

  • Black widow: shiny black with a red hourglass under the belly. Bite may cause severe muscle cramps, belly pain, sweating, and nausea within hours.
  • Brown recluse: brown with a violin shape on its back. Bite may become a painful sore that spreads over days.
  • For either: wash, apply cold, keep the area still, and get medical care.

Scorpions

Most US scorpion stings cause local pain only. The bark scorpion in the Southwest can cause numbness, muscle twitching, odd eye movements, and trouble breathing, especially in children. Get medical care for anything beyond local pain.

Marine stings

  • Jellyfish: get out of the water. Rinse with seawater (not fresh water). Remove tentacles with tweezers or a gloved hand. Hot water, as hot as the person can stand without burning, may ease pain.
  • Stingray or spiny fish: soak in hot water (not scalding) for pain and get care. These wounds often need cleaning by a professional.
For itching from mild stings, an over-the-counter antihistamine or hydrocortisone cream can help. Follow the label.
