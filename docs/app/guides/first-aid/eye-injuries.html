Do this now

  1. Chemical in the eye: flush with clean, lukewarm running water right away for at least 15 to 20 minutes. Hold the eye open. Tilt the head so water runs away from the other eye. Then call 911 or Poison Control.
  2. Object stuck in the eye: don't remove it. Cover it with a paper cup or shield taped in place without pressing on the eye. Get emergency care.
  3. Blow to the eye: put a cold pack on gently for 15 minutes without pressing. Get care if vision changes.
  4. Small particle like dust or sand: blink, let tears wash it, or flush with clean water. Don't rub.
  5. Get emergency care for any cut to the eye, bleeding in the eye, vision loss, or bad pain.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Tips

  • Remove contact lenses if they don't come out during flushing, but don't delay flushing to do it.
  • Covering both eyes can help keep the injured eye still, because eyes move together. Tell the person what you are doing and guide them by the arm.
  • Don't put pressure on an eye that may be cut or punctured.
  • Don't use eye drops or ointment on a serious injury unless a provider says to.

Danger signs

  • Vision loss, double vision, or flashes of light.
  • Blood in the colored part or the white of the eye after a hit.
  • Pupil that is an odd shape.
  • Severe pain, or the eye won't open.
  • Any chemical splash, especially strong cleaners or lye.

Smoke and ash

Wildfire smoke and ash can irritate the eyes. Rinse with clean water. Wear goggles during cleanup. Don't rub.
