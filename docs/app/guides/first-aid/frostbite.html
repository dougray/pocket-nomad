Do this now

  1. Get the person to a warm place. Check for hypothermia first. It is more dangerous and needs care first.
  2. Remove wet clothes and anything tight, like rings or watches.
  3. Do not rub the frozen area, and do not rub it with snow.
  4. Don't thaw it if it could freeze again before you reach help. Thawing then refreezing causes much worse damage.
  5. To rewarm: soak the area in warm, not hot, water, about 99 to 104 °F (37 to 40 °C). It should feel comfortable to an unhurt hand. Or use body heat, like fingers in an armpit.
  6. Put dry, clean gauze between fingers and toes. Loosely wrap the area.
  7. Get medical care.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Signs

  • Frostnip (early): red, cold, tingling, or numb skin. Goes back to normal when warmed.
  • Frostbite: skin is white, grayish-yellow, or waxy. It feels hard or numb. Blisters may form after rewarming.

Fingers, toes, nose, ears, cheeks, and chin freeze first.

Do not

  • Don't use a heating pad, heat lamp, stove, fireplace, or radiator to warm frostbite. Numb skin burns easily.
  • Don't walk on frostbitten feet or toes if you can avoid it.
  • Don't break blisters.
Rewarming frostbite is very painful. The skin may swell, turn red, purple, or blister. That is expected. Over-the-counter pain medicine can help. Follow the label.
