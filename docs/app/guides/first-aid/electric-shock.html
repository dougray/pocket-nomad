Do this now

  1. Don't touch the person if they may still be in contact with electricity.
  2. Turn off the power at the breaker or unplug the device if you can do so safely.
  3. For downed power lines, stay well away, at least 35 ft (10 m), and call 911 and the power company. Assume every line is live.
  4. Once the power is off, check breathing. If not breathing normally, start CPR and use an AED.
  5. Call 911 for any shock from high voltage or lightning, or any shock causing burns, confusion, or heart symptoms.
  6. Cool any burns with running water and cover them loosely.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Separating someone from a low-voltage source

If you cannot turn off the power, and only for household voltage: stand on something dry and non-metal (like a rubber mat or dry board). Use a dry wooden broom handle or similar non-metal object to push the source away. Never do this with high-voltage lines.

Lightning

  • People struck by lightning do not carry a charge. It is safe to touch them right away.
  • Move to a safer place if lightning is still active nearby.
  • Lightning often stops the heart and breathing. Start CPR right away. People struck by lightning have a good chance of recovery with fast CPR.
  • If several people are struck, help those who seem dead first. This is different from regular triage.

Why get checked

Electricity can cause deep burns, broken bones from muscle spasms, and heart rhythm problems that start later. Small skin marks can hide big injuries inside. Anyone with more than a minor household shock should see a doctor.

Car in contact with a power line

If you are in a car touching a downed line, stay inside and call 911. If you must leave because of fire, jump clear with both feet together, never touching the car and ground at the same time. Then shuffle away with small steps, keeping your feet close together.
