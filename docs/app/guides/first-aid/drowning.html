Do this now

  1. Reach, throw, don't go. Reach with a pole or branch, or throw a float, rope, or cooler. Don't swim out unless you are trained. A panicking person can pull you under.
  2. Call 911, or have someone else call.
  3. Once they are out of the water, check breathing.
  4. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR with rescue breaths. Breaths are especially important for drowning.
  5. If they vomit, roll them on their side, clear the mouth, and continue.
  6. If they are breathing, put them on their side and keep them warm.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

What drowning looks like

Drowning is usually quiet. The person often can't wave or yell. Watch for:

  • Head low in the water, mouth at water level.
  • Head tilted back with mouth open.
  • Body upright, not using the legs, as if climbing a ladder.
  • Eyes glassy or closed. Hair over the eyes.

After a near-drowning

Anyone who was pulled from the water after struggling, coughed a lot, or needed breaths should be checked at a hospital, even if they seem fine. Breathing problems can show up hours later.

Flood water and ice

  • Never walk or drive into moving flood water. 6 in (15 cm) of moving water can knock an adult down.
  • For someone through the ice: lie flat to spread your weight, and reach with a pole, rope, or ladder. Don't stand at the edge of the hole.
  • Cold water causes gasping and can stop a strong swimmer within minutes. Treat for hypothermia once they are out.

Don't

  • Don't press on the belly to get water out. It wastes time and can cause vomiting.
  • Don't delay CPR to drain water.
