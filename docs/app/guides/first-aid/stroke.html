Do this now

  1. Use BE FAST to check for a stroke (see below).
  2. If you see any sign, call 911 right away. Every minute counts. Treatment works best in the first few hours.
  3. Note the time the signs started, or the last time the person was seen normal. Tell the medical team.
  4. Keep the person lying on their side with the head slightly raised, or in a comfortable position. Loosen tight clothing.
  5. Don't give food, drink, or any medicine, including aspirin. They may not be able to swallow safely.
  6. If they become unresponsive and stop breathing normally, start CPR.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

BE FAST

LetterCheck
BalanceSudden dizziness, loss of balance, or trouble walking.
EyesSudden loss of vision, blurred vision, or double vision in one or both eyes.
FaceAsk them to smile. Does one side of the face droop?
ArmsAsk them to raise both arms. Does one arm drift down, or is one side weak or numb?
SpeechAsk them to repeat a simple sentence. Is speech slurred, strange, or hard to understand?
TimeTime to call 911. Note the time signs began.

Other sudden signs

  • Severe headache with no known cause, often called "the worst headache of my life."
  • Confusion or trouble understanding.
  • Numbness or weakness, often on one side of the body.
Even if the signs go away within a few minutes, call 911. This may be a "mini-stroke" (TIA), which is a warning that a major stroke may follow.
