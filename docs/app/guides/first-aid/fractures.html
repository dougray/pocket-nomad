Do this now

  1. If the bone sticks out or there is heavy bleeding, call 911 and control bleeding first. Press around the bone, not on it.
  2. Don't move the injured part unless you must. Don't try to push a bone back in or straighten it.
  3. Support the injury in the position you found it.
  4. Splint it if you need to move the person. The splint must cover the joint above and below the break.
  5. Check fingers or toes before and after splinting. Look for warmth, color, and feeling.
  6. Put a cold pack wrapped in cloth on the injury for up to 20 minutes.
  7. Call 911 for breaks of the thigh, hip, pelvis, neck, back, or skull, or if fingers or toes are cold, blue, or numb.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Signs of a broken bone

  • Bad pain, swelling, and bruising. Pain when touched in one spot.
  • The limb is bent at a strange angle or is shorter than the other side.
  • A snap or grinding sound or feeling.
  • The person can't use or put weight on it.

You can't always tell a break from a bad sprain. If in doubt, treat it as a break.

How to splint

  1. Find something stiff: a board, rolled-up newspaper or magazine, a trekking pole, a sleeping pad, or a stick.
  2. Pad the splint with cloth, especially around bony spots.
  3. Place it along the injured part so it reaches past the joint above and below the break.
  4. Tie it on with cloth strips, belts, or tape. Tie above and below the injury, not right on it.
  5. Make it snug but not tight. You should be able to slip a finger under the ties.
  6. Check the fingers or toes again. If they turn pale, blue, cold, numb, or tingly, loosen the ties.

Quick splints

  • Arm: splint the forearm, then put it in a sling (a triangle of cloth tied around the neck). Add a second strip around the chest to hold the arm against the body.
  • Leg: tie the injured leg to the good leg with padding between them (buddy splint).
  • Finger or toe: tape it to the one next to it, with gauze between them.
Thigh, hip, and pelvis breaks can cause heavy bleeding inside the body and shock. Keep the person still, lying down, and warm. Call 911.
If you suspect the neck or back is hurt, don't move the person. See the Spinal Injury guide.
