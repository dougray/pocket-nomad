Do this now

  1. Call 911 if the person passed out, is confused, is vomiting, has a seizure, or has any danger sign below.
  2. Keep the person still. If they might have a neck injury, hold their head in line with their body. Don't move them unless they're in danger.
  3. Control scalp bleeding with gentle pressure. If the skull feels soft or dented, press around the edges, not on it.
  4. If clear fluid or blood comes from the nose or ears, don't try to stop it. Cover it loosely.
  5. Watch them closely for at least 24 hours. Danger signs can show up hours later.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Danger signs: get emergency care now

  • One pupil (the black center of the eye) is bigger than the other.
  • Very drowsy, or you can't wake them.
  • Headache that gets worse and won't go away.
  • Repeated vomiting.
  • Seizures.
  • Slurred speech, weakness, numbness, or poor balance.
  • Getting more confused, restless, or agitated. Can't recognize people or places.
  • Passed out, even for a moment.
  • Clear fluid from the nose or ears.

Concussion signs

A concussion is a brain injury from a blow or jolt to the head. Most people don't pass out. Signs include:

  • Headache or pressure in the head.
  • Dizziness, balance problems, or blurry or double vision.
  • Bothered by light or noise.
  • Feeling foggy, slowed down, or confused.
  • Can't remember what happened right before or after the injury.
  • Nausea. Mood changes. Trouble sleeping.

Care

  • Stop all activity. When in doubt, sit them out. No sports or hard work until a health care provider clears them.
  • Let them rest. It's OK to let them sleep if they are awake and talking normally and have no danger signs. Check on them during the first night.
  • No alcohol or drugs.
  • Don't leave them alone for the first day.
Older adults and people on blood thinners can bleed inside the skull after even a small bump. Have them checked by a doctor.
