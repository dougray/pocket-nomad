Do this now

  1. Get the person out of the cold and wind. Get them into shelter if you can.
  2. Handle them gently. Rough handling can trigger a dangerous heart rhythm in severe hypothermia.
  3. Remove wet clothing. Replace it with dry layers, or wrap them in blankets, a sleeping bag, and a plastic or foil sheet. Cover the head and neck.
  4. Insulate them from the ground.
  5. Warm the center of the body first: chest, neck, armpits, and groin. Use warm (not hot) packs or bottles wrapped in cloth. Skin-to-skin contact helps only if you have nothing else.
  6. If they are fully awake, shivering, and can swallow, give a sweet drink or food. Warm is good, but not hot enough to burn. No alcohol: it makes the body lose heat faster.
  7. Call 911. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR.
Call 911 (or your local emergency number) as soon as you can. If you are alone with an adult, call first. If you can, put the phone on speaker so the dispatcher can coach you. This guide does not replace hands-on first aid training. Take a class from the Red Cross or American Heart Association when you can.

Signs

StageSigns
MildShivering, cold pale skin, clumsy hands, slurred speech, poor judgment ("the umbles": stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, grumbles).
Moderate to severeShivering slows or stops. Confusion, very sleepy, slow breathing, weak pulse. May pass out.
When a cold person stops shivering without getting warmer, it is a very bad sign. Get help fast. A very cold person can look dead and still survive. Check for breathing and a pulse for a full minute, because pulses are hard to feel in the cold. If there are none, start CPR and keep going until help takes over. Short pauses to move them are OK. Don't start CPR if the chest is frozen too stiff to press or the injuries are clearly fatal.

Do not

  • Don't rub or massage arms and legs.
  • Don't use direct heat like hot water, a heating pad on bare skin, or a fire close by. It can burn numb skin.
  • Don't put a person who is confused, very sleepy, or not shivering in a warm bath or shower. It can make their blood pressure drop.
  • Don't give alcohol.

Who is at risk

Hypothermia can happen above freezing, especially when someone is wet, in wind, or in cool water. Babies, older adults, people who drank alcohol, and anyone without heat during a winter power outage are at high risk. Babies may have bright red, cold skin and very low energy.

To stay warm: stay dry, wear layers (wool or synthetic, not cotton), cover your head, eat and drink, and get out of the wind.
