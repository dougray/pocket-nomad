Do this now

  1. Life in danger and no phone? Pick up any radio you have and call for help in plain language on a channel people listen to (see the table below).
  2. Listen first for a few seconds so you don't talk over someone.
  3. Press and hold the talk button, wait 1 second, then speak slowly and clearly, with the radio 1 to 2 in (3 to 5 cm) from your mouth.
  4. Say: "Emergency, emergency, emergency." Then who you are, where you are, what happened, and what help you need.
  5. Release the button and listen. Repeat every few minutes. Try other channels if no one answers.
  6. Get high: climb a hill or go upstairs. Radio range grows a lot with height.
Frequencies, nets, and rules here are for the United States. Other countries use different rules. Nets and local channel plans change over time, so confirm them with local radio groups when you can.

The emergency rule

Normally you need an FCC license to transmit on ham and GMRS frequencies. FCC ham rules (47 CFR 97.403 and 97.405) let a licensed ham station use any means of radio communication it has when there is an immediate threat to human life or property and normal systems are down. These rules do not license anyone else.

If you have no license, use FRS, CB, or Meshtastic first. A GMRS licensee may let anyone use their radio to send an emergency message. Transmitting on ham bands without a license is a last resort, for a true life-or-death emergency only.

This is for real, immediate danger to life when phones and other normal ways are down. It is not permission to chat, coordinate supplies, or use radios without a license for convenience. Those rules are written for amateur stations; use the exception only for a true distress call, then follow the normal rules once the danger has passed.

Radio range, roughly

  • Handheld radios on flat ground: often 1 to 2 miles (2 to 3 km). Less in cities, forests, and buildings.
  • From hilltops or high buildings: much farther.
  • Through a ham or GMRS repeater on a tower or hill: often 20 miles (30 km) or more.
  • HF ham radio can reach across the country or the world, but needs a General license and bigger antennas.

Which radio to get

  • Listen only: a NOAA Weather Radio, and an AM/FM radio for news. No license needed.
  • Family talk, no license: FRS radios or CB.
  • Family talk, longer range: GMRS (license, no exam).
  • Best reach and community help: ham radio (license with exam).
  • Text messages without cell service, no license: Meshtastic LoRa devices.
