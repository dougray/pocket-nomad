Do this now

  1. Listen first. Make sure no one else is talking.
  2. Press the talk button. Wait 1 second, then speak. Radios cut off the first word if you rush.
  3. Say who you are calling, then who you are: "Base, this is Mobile 2." On ham or GMRS, use call signs.
  4. Keep it short. Think before you press. Release the button after each thought so others can talk.
  5. End with "Over" (your turn) or "Out" (done). Not both.

Emergency and priority calls

What to sayWhen
"MAYDAY" x3Life in immediate danger. Standard on marine and aviation radio, and understood everywhere. On FRS and GMRS, say "Emergency" instead, unless you are on a boat, plane, or vehicle in immediate danger.
"PAN-PAN" x3Urgent, but no immediate danger to life. Marine and aviation.
"Emergency, emergency, emergency"Plain-language emergency call on ham, GMRS, FRS, CB. Clear to anyone.
"Emergency traffic" or "Priority traffic"On a net or busy repeater: you have an urgent message. Wait for a gap between transmissions, say it with your call sign, and wait to be told to go ahead.
"Break"On ham repeaters, used between other people's transmissions to ask for a turn. Some areas use "Break break" for urgent traffic. Habits vary by area.

If you hear an emergency call, stop talking and listen. Answer only if you can help or relay. Write down what you hear.

What to say in an emergency call

  1. Who you are (name or call sign).
  2. Where you are: address, cross streets, mile marker, landmark, or latitude and longitude. Say it twice.
  3. What happened.
  4. How many people are hurt, and how badly.
  5. What you need: ambulance, fire, rescue, water, shelter.
  6. Ask them to read it back to confirm.

Good habits

  • Use plain language. No 10-codes or slang. Different groups use different codes.
  • Spell names, street names, and call signs using the phonetic alphabet ("Main: Mike Alfa India November").
  • Say numbers digit by digit: "one four six five two zero."
  • Use 24-hour time: "fourteen thirty" for 2:30 p.m.
  • Write down messages you send and receive, with the time.
  • Don't talk over a net control station. Wait to be called on.
